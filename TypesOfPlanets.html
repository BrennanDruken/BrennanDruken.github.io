Types of Planets

Super-Jupiter: A planet more massive than the planet Jupiter.

Giant Planet: A massive planet, typically composed primarily of gas (hydrogen & helium) or ices (water, methane & ammonia), but may also be composed primarily of rock, which would make one a Mega-Earth.

Super-Neptune: A planet that is more massive than the planet Neptune. Super-Neptunes are generally described as being around 5-7 times the diameter of Earth.

Neptunian Planet: Planets of mass similar to Uranus or Neptune; smaller than the gas giants in our solar system, but still much larger than Earth.

Sub-Neptune: A planet with a smaller diameter than Neptune, even if it has a larger mass.

Mini-Neptune: Also known as a gas dwarf, a Mini-Neptune is a planet less massive than Uranus and Neptune. Mini-Neptunes have thick hydrogen-helium atmospheres.

Mega-Earth: A massive terrestrial planet, at least 10x the mass of Earth.

Super-Earth: A planet with a diameter and mass higher than Earth's, but a lot smaller than Uranus and Neptune.

Sub-Earth: A planet substantially less massive than Earth and Venus.
